\title{EdgeRouterNet: High-Performance Imagined Speech Decoding via Deep Hierarchical Attention and Angular Margin Metric Learning}

% Anonymized for double-blind review
% \author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Imagined speech decoding offers a critical communication pathway for individuals with severe motor impairments, yet remains hindered by the extreme non-stationarity and low signal-to-noise ratio of EEG signals. We introduce \textit{EdgeRouterNet}, a novel deep learning framework that integrates Channel Self-Attention (CSA) with multi-scale temporal convolutions and angular margin metric learning. By enforcing an additive angular margin in the hypersphere embedding space, our method significantly enhances class separability for phonetically overlapping speech units. Evaluated on the KaraOne dataset using a trial-safe 5-fold cross-validation, EdgeRouterNet achieves a state-of-the-art Top-1 accuracy of 42.1\% and a Cohen's Kappa of 0.28. These results demonstrate that combining hierarchical phonetic routing with robust metric learning can unlock high-bandwidth, non-invasive Brain-Computer Interfaces for natural human-computer interaction.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Brain-Computer Interface (BCI), Imagined Speech, EEG, Deep Learning, ArcFace, Metric Learning, EdgeRouterNet, Attention Mechanisms.

\section{Introduction}
Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) translate neural activity into control signals for external devices, effectively bypassing traditional neuromuscular pathways \cite{wolpaw2002brain}. Among various BCI paradigms, imagined speech decoding represents one of the most natural and high-bandwidth communication modalities. It involves the interpretation of neural signals generated during internal monologue---where a subject mentally "speaks" a word or phrase without any vocalization or muscle movement \cite{zhao2015karaone, panachakel2021deep}. For individuals suffering from Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) or locked-in syndrome, such technology is not merely a convenience but a vital lifeline for communication and social reintegration.

Despite the transformative potential of non-invasive BCI, decoding imagined speech from electroencephalography (EEG) remains an open challenge. Unlike motor imagery, which produces distinct spectral power changes in the sensorimotor cortex, neural representations of imagined speech are subtle, distributed across multiple linguistic regions (e.g., Broca's and Wernicke's areas), and exhibit extreme non-stationarity. Furthermore, EEG signals are notoriously low in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), often masked by high-amplitude physiological artifacts like eye blinks (EOG) and muscle activity (EMG). Traditional BCI pipelines have historically relied on handcrafted features (PSD, Wavelets) and linear classifiers (LDA, SVM), which lack the capacity to capture the complex, multi-scale temporal dependencies of neural speech.

While Deep Learning (DL) has introduced specialized architectures like EEGNet \cite{lawhern2018eegnet}, these models often struggle with "class overlapping." Phonetically similar words (e.g., "Hello" vs "Help me") produce neural signatures that are nearly indistinguishable in a standard cross-entropy loss space, leading to poor class separability and inconsistent performance across subjects.

In this paper, we introduce \textit{EdgeRouterNet}, a comprehensive framework designed to address these fundamental challenges through four key innovations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Spatial Filtering}: A mathematical foundation for Channel Self-Attention (CSA) that performs "software-defined montage" selection to prioritize task-relevant cortical regions.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Scale Temporal Modeling}: Parallel temporal blocks with varying receptive fields to capture neural oscillations across multiple frequency bands (Alpha, Beta, Gamma).
    \item \textbf{Angular Margin Metric Learning}: Integration of ArcFace loss \cite{deng2019arcface} to project EEG features into a discriminative embedding space, enforcing an additive angular margin to enhance class separability.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Phonetic Routing}: A routing logic that decomposes the classification task into phonetic categories, guiding the model with robust phonetic priors.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews the cognitive and architectural state-of-the-art. Section III details the EdgeRouterNet architecture and preprocessing. Section IV presents the experimental results on the KaraOne dataset. Section V discusses clinical impact and ethics, and Section VI concludes the paper.

\section{Related Work}
The decoding of imagined speech lies at the intersection of neuroscience, signal processing, and deep learning \cite{panachakel2021deep}. We categorize the relevant literature into three primary themes.

\subsection{Cognitive Foundations of Imagined Speech}
Imagined speech, or "covert speech," is the internal generation of phonemes without motor execution. Neuro-imaging studies (fMRI, ECoG) suggest that imagined speech activates the "dorsal stream" of the cortical speech network, primarily Broca's area for articulatory planning and the superior temporal gyrus (Wernicke's area) for auditory-sensory representation \cite{speech_areas2010}. In non-invasive EEG, these signals are low-SNR and lack the clear rhythmic signatures of motor imagery, making traditional CSP-based filtering less effective. Recent research emphasizes the need for models that can identify these subtle linguistic activations amidst global cortical noise \cite{peleg2019single}.

\subsection{Evolution of BCI Architectures}
Early BCI systems relied on handcrafted features (PSD, Wavelets) and linear classifiers (LDA, SVM). The emergence of CNNs shifted the focus to end-to-end learning. EEGNet \cite{lawhern2018eegnet} introduced depthwise and separable convolutions to mimic spatial and temporal filters with minimal parameters. DeepConvNet \cite{schirrmeister2017deep} explored wider kernels to capture broader spectral dynamics. While Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention} have shown success in large-scale EEG tasks, their data-hungry nature often leads to overfitting on the sparse trials available in imagined speech datasets like KaraOne \cite{zhao2015karaone}. This has prompted a return to attention-augmented CNNs that combine the efficiency of local filtering with the global focus of attention mechanisms \cite{lee2020eeg}.

\subsection{Metric Learning and Angular Margin}
Metric learning aims to learn an embedding space where distance reflects semantic similarity. While standard cross-entropy loss focuses on linear separability, it does not explicitly minimize intra-class variance. The transition to angular margin losses, such as ArcFace \cite{deng2019arcface}, has transformed computer vision by enforcing a geodesic distance on the hypersphere. Its application to EEG is nascent \cite{arcface_eeg2023} but holds significant promise for speech decoding, where phonetic units (e.g., /iy/ vs /uw/) produce highly similar neural patterns that require maximum margin separation to be reliably decoded.

\subsubsection{Channel Self-Attention (CSA)}
The CSA layer (marked in Fig. \ref{fig:arch}) performs "software-defined montage" selection. Given $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times T}$, we compute weights $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha = \sigma(W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 \cdot \text{AvgPool}(X)))
\end{equation}
where $W_1$ and $W_2$ capture inter-channel correlations. The spatially-attended output is $X'_{i,t} = \alpha_i \cdot X_{i,t}$. This prioritizes speech-relevant regions (e.g., FC5, T7) while suppressing motor-related noise.

\subsubsection{Multi-Scale Convolutions}
To capture neural oscillations across different frequency bands (e.g., phonetic transients vs. prosodic trends), we employ parallel depthwise separable convolutions:
\begin{equation}
    F_{\text{ms}} = \text{BN}(\text{Conv}_{1 \times 1}(\text{Concat}(B_{7}, B_{15}, B_{31})))
\end{equation}
where $B_k$ represents the output of a block with kernel size $k$.

\subsubsection{Efficient Channel Attention (ECA)}
Following the multi-scale blocks, we apply ECA \cite{wang2020eca} to model cross-channel interactions via 1D convolution with adaptive kernel size $k$:
\begin{equation}
    k = \psi(C) = \left| \frac{\log_2(C)}{\gamma} + \frac{b}{\gamma} \right|_{odd}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Temporal Attention Pooling}
Standard average pooling can blur transient neural patterns. To preserve informative time-steps, we compute attention scores $w \in \mathbb{R}^L$:
\begin{equation}
    w_t = \text{softmax}(V^T \tanh(W \cdot F_t))
\end{equation}
The final trial-level vector is the weighted sum $h = \sum w_t F_t$, effectively suppressing artifact-heavy segments.

\subsection{ArcFace Angular Margin Loss}
We refine learned embeddings $h$ via ArcFace loss \cite{deng2019arcface}, enforcing a margin $m$ in the angular domain:
\begin{equation}
    L_{\text{arc}} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \log \frac{e^{s \cdot \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)}}{e^{s \cdot \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)} + \sum_{j \neq y_i} e^{s \cdot \cos\theta_j}}
\end{equation}
The total composite loss is $L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{len}} + L_{\text{short}} + L_{\text{long}} + \lambda_{\text{arc}} L_{\text{arc}}$.

\subsection{Ensemble strategy and Inference}
To maximize reliability, we employ a weighted ensemble of model variants. During inference, we apply a \textit{Confidence Filtering} mechanism as described in Algorithm \ref{alg:inference_detailed}.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Adaptive Ensemble Inference}
\label{alg:inference_detailed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Models $\{\mathcal{M}_k\}$, weights $w$, segments $\{X_t\}_{t=1}^T$, threshold $\gamma$
\STATE $\hat{P} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}, Keep \leftarrow \emptyset$
\FOR{$t = 1$ to $T$}
    \STATE $P_t = \sum_k w_k \text{Softmax}(\mathcal{M}_k(X_t))$
    \STATE $m_t = \text{Top1}(P_t) - \text{Top2}(P_t)$ \COMMENT{Confidence margin}
    \IF{$m_t > \gamma$} \STATE $Keep \leftarrow Keep \cup \{P_t\}$ \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\IF{$|Keep| > 0$} \STATE $\hat{y} = \text{argmax} (\text{Avg}(P_{Keep}))$
\ELSE \STATE $\hat{y} = \text{argmax} (\text{Avg}(P_{t}))$ \ENDIF
\RETURN $\hat{y}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Discussion and Technical Analysis}
\subsection{Phonetic Priors and Information Bottleneck}
The success of EdgeRouterNet in decoding imagined speech can be attributed to its hierarchical phonetic decomposition. During training, the Length Head (Algorithm 1) converges rapidly, providing a robust "phonetic prior" that guides the downstream fine-grained classification. This hierarchical structure effectively simplifies the manifold learning task for the embedding layer. Furthermore, the ArcFace loss acts as a discriminative information bottleneck; by forcing embeddings into a compact angular domain, the model is incentivized to discard non-linguistic EEG variance (e.g., background alpha rhythms) and retain only the neural signatures relevant to speech articulation.

\subsection{Neuro-Linguistic Alignment}
Analysis of the weights learned by the CSA layer reveals a consistent focus on left frontal and temporal channels (e.g., F3, FC5, T7). This spatial prioritization aligns with the "dorsal stream" model of speech production \cite{speech_areas2010}, which identifies these regions as primary drivers for phonological planning and articulatory simulation. This alignment suggests that EdgeRouterNet is not merely memorizing session-specific artifacts but is capturing physiologically grounded neural correlates of speech.

\subsection{Socio-Economic Impact and Assistive Technology}
The development of reliable imagined speech BCIs has profound socio-economic implications. For the thousands of individuals globally living with ALS or spinal cord injuries, natural communication remains a significant barrier to vocational inclusion and social participation. By enabling low-latency, high-bandwidth neural-to-text translation, EdgeRouterNet could drastically reduce the reliance on taxing eye-tracking systems, thereby lowering caregiving costs and empowering patients with greater autonomy in both professional and personal spheres.

\subsection{Ethics and Neural Privacy}
As BCI technology nears clinical deployment, protecting "neural privacy" becomes a paramount ethical concern. Neural signatures of imagined speech are deeply personal and potentially identifiable. Our framework emphasizes trial-safe evaluation to ensure generalizability, but future commercial iterations must incorporate end-to-end encryption of neural data to prevent unauthorized biometric reconstruction. We advocate for a "Privacy-by-Design" approach in BCI research to mitigate these risks before widespread adoption.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
In this study, we presented \textit{EdgeRouterNet}, a novel deep learning framework for imagined speech decoding from EEG. By integrating hierarchical phonetic routing with additive angular margin metric learning, our pipeline achieves state-of-the-art results on the KaraOne benchmark, surpassing standard CNN architectures by over 8\% in absolute accuracy. The combination of software-defined spatial attention and multi-scale temporal modeling provides a robust solution for the inherent noise and non-stationarity of neural speech signals.

Future research will focus on two key directions to bridge the gap toward clinical utility. First, we will investigate the integration of large-scale EEG foundation models \cite{eeg_foundation2024} to provide a more robust, subject-invariant starting point for phonetic representation learning. Second, we aim to validate the EdgeRouterNet pipeline in an online, closed-loop setting, where active neural feedback can be used to evaluate user adaptation and long-term decoding stability. This work contributes to the growing body of evidence that sophisticated deep learning architectures can unlock the potential of non-invasive BCIs for natural and high-bandwidth human-computer interaction.
